WHAT EXACTLY IS CyberSafety ?

Cyber safety refers to the practice of safeguarding personal and sensitive information when using digital devices and the internet. It encompasses strategies and precautions taken to protect users from cyber threats and ensure secure interactions online

WHY IS PROTECTING DATA SO IMPORTANT?

Protecting your data is essential to safeguard privacy, prevent unauthorized access, maintain trust, and comply with legal requirements. Privacy and Personal Rights Data protection ensures that your personal information, such as names, addresses, financial details, and online behavior, remains private and under your control. Privacy is a fundamental right, allowing individuals to decide who can access their information and how it is used, which is increasingly important in a digital world where data is constantly collected and shared (Staysafeonline.org)

A LITTLE DEEPER INTO EACH CATEGORIES

Refers to malicious software designed to damage, disrupt or gain unauthorized access to computer systems or take control of a system without the user's knowledge.

A social engineering technique where attackers trick users into revealing sensitive information such as passwords, banking details or login credentials. Attackers send fake emails, SMS or messages pretending to be trusted companies, redirecting victims to fake websites that look like real login pages.

A DDoS attack attempts to make a website or online service unavailable by flooding it with massive traffic from multiple systems. Attackers use thousands of infected devices called a botnet, so legitimate users cannot access the website or service. Example: a botnet floods a web server with millions of HTTP requests, causing service downtime.

Ransomware is a type of malware that encrypts a victim's files or locks their computer system. The attacker demands a ransom payment to restore access. Usually spreads through phishing emails or malicious downloads. Example: WannaCry ransomware encrypts system files and demands Bitcoin payment for decryption.

A web application attack where attackers insert malicious SQL commands into input fields to manipulate the database. Sensitive data like usernames and passwords may be exposed. Example: an attacker enters ' OR '1'='1 in a login form to bypass database authentication.

Exploits a previously unknown vulnerability in software before developers release a patch or fix. Developers are unaware of the flaw when the attack occurs. Example: attackers exploit an unpatched browser vulnerability before the vendor releases a security update.

An attacker secretly intercepts communication between two parties, often on unsecured public Wi-Fi, to monitor, steal, or alter transmitted data. Example: a hacker intercepts user credentials over unsecured public Wi-Fi using packet sniffing.

Attackers attempt to obtain or crack a user's password using automated tools, including brute force and dictionary attacks. Example: a brute-force tool repeatedly tests password combinations to gain unauthorized SSH access.
